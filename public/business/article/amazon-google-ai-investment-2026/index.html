Why Amazon and Google Are Increasing Their Investment in AI

Both tech giants are pouring capital into data centers, custom silicon, and frontier startups to lock in cloud workloads and protect their core profit engines.

DL
David Lindholm
Tech Markets Editor
Nov 17, 2026 • 3 min read

Amazon and Alphabet have spent 2026 ratcheting up capital expenditures and strategic equity commitments across artificial intelligence, undeterred by growing Wall Street anxiety over short-term free cash flow. Their aggressive posture reflects a straightforward calculation at the board level: underinvesting in compute capacity or model distribution carries far greater existential risk than temporarily compressing operating margins.

Securing cloud workloads and reducing chip dependence

For Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud, AI infrastructure has become the primary engine of enterprise contract expansion. Frontier model developers now sign multi-year compute agreements measured in hundreds of billions of dollars, as illustrated by the roughly $900.9 billion in long-term compute commitments Anthropic disclosed ahead of its November IPO. Hosting those training runs and inference endpoints pulls adjacent spending in storage, networking, databases, and security onto the same cloud platform.

At the same time, both companies are spending heavily on in-house silicon, such as Amazon's Trainium and Inferentia chips and Google's Tensor Processing Units. Nvidia's 74.8% GAAP gross margin in its latest quarter shows how much economic value flows to external chip suppliers. Building viable custom accelerators gives Amazon and Google leverage over pricing while tailoring hardware directly to their internal recommendation, logistics, and search systems.

Strategic bets beyond pure infrastructure

Alphabet is also deploying its balance sheet into high-value vertical applications where AI can create proprietary intellectual property. On November 17, 2026, its DeepMind spinout Isomorphic Labs closed a record $5 billion Series C round with backing from Alphabet, GV, CapitalG, Sequoia Capital, and Greenoaks to expand its AI drug discovery pipeline. Meanwhile, Amazon is embedding generative models across retail search, warehouse robotics, and advertising targeting.

Investors rightly question how quickly these outlays will produce matching profits. Still, both companies view today's spending spree as the entry fee to own the default operating layer of enterprise computing for the next decade.
