Why Are Technology Stocks Falling? Understanding the Latest Market Sell-Off

Tighter US export rules, executive turnover at Nvidia, and pointed Federal Reserve skepticism combined to push the Nasdaq into correction territory.

Technology shares endured a brisk sell-off in November 2026, with the Nasdaq sliding roughly 2.5% in a single session and entering official correction territory. The retreat caught many retail investors off guard because underlying earnings from major AI suppliers remain exceptionally strong. Instead of collapsing fundamentals, the drawdown reflects three sudden risk factors hitting a market priced for perfection.

Export restrictions and executive uncertainty

Policy risk triggered the first wave of institutional selling. The U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security's November interim final rule tightened controls on remote cloud access for restricted D:5 entities and added licensing hurdles for advanced AI server shipments to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, and Brazil. Because rapid international data center expansion has been a core pillar of semiconductor growth models, analysts quickly trimmed near-term regional shipment forecasts.

Compounding that anxiety was unexpected leadership change at the sector's bellwether. Jensen Huang's exit from Nvidia on November 12 left CFO Colette Kress stepping in as interim CEO and Harvey Jones taking over as board chair. Even though Nvidia went on to report $115.8 billion in Q3 FY2027 revenue days later, institutional funds routinely reduce exposure when an iconic founder leaves an enterprise worth trillions.

Central bank scrutiny over AI payback

Macroeconomic commentary delivered the final blow to risk appetite. Federal Reserve officials publicly questioned whether the hundreds of billions flowing into AI data centers will translate into timely productivity gains and corporate profits. Those remarks reminded equity markets that higher capital intensity carries real balance-sheet risk if borrowing costs remain elevated.

With Anthropic also preparing to absorb tens of billions in public market liquidity through its Nasdaq listing, portfolio managers have trimmed existing winners to rebalance exposure. For now, the sell-off looks less like an end to AI adoption and more like a sharper repricing of geopolitical, governance, and capital-return risks.
