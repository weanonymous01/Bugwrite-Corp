Salesforce unveils Microsoft Copilotforce partnership amid fresh job cuts and internal AI budgets

The CRM giant is tightening internal spending and addressing third-party security ripples ahead of its December 2 earnings report.

DL
David Lindholm
Business & Markets Editor
Nov 18, 2026 • 5 min read

Salesforce is navigating a turbulent stretch in mid-November 2026, unveiling a landmark AI partnership with Microsoft on November 17 while simultaneously carrying out a fresh round of employee layoffs and clamping down on internal generative AI spending. The contrasting headlines capture the pressure facing enterprise software leaders as Wall Street demands clear revenue growth from costly AI pivots.

The headline product announcement is Copilotforce, a joint initiative linking Salesforce customer insights and Data 360 directly with Microsoft Copilot, Teams, and Microsoft Fabric. Built around two-way Zero Copy integrations, the partnership is designed to let large enterprises trigger automated workflows across both ecosystems without duplicating sensitive customer databases.

Job cuts and monthly AI token limits

Behind the scenes, however, Salesforce is tightening its belt. Internal staff communications reviewed by Business Insider confirmed another wave of workforce reductions in mid-November, adding to a difficult year of ongoing restructuring even though management has not publicly detailed the exact headcount affected.

At the same time, the company introduced role-based monthly AI token budgets for its own workforce after discovering that some employees were running up thousands of dollars a month in generative AI usage costs. The move underscores a growing reality across Silicon Valley: even companies selling autonomous AI agents to corporate buyers are having to meter their own inference bills.

Third-party security ripples and Wall Street jitters

Salesforce also dealt with two third-party ecosystem security incidents this month. After a supply chain exploit hit Optimizely's web experimentation platform across roughly 178 major brands, Salesforce temporarily disabled Optimizely on its web properties as a precaution, noting that its core platform remained untouched. Separately, Salesforce temporarily disabled ComplianceQuest managed packages after detecting suspicious third-party token activity, rolling safe functionality back out following security remediations.

Investors have stayed cautious. Salesforce shares slid roughly 9 percent during a broader mid-November software selloff, with analysts questioning how quickly CEO Marc Benioff's Agentforce push will translate into durable subscription revenue. Wall Street will get its next hard look on Wednesday, December 2, 2026, when Salesforce reports Q3 Fiscal 2027 earnings after the market close.
