Anthropic IPO: What Investors Should Know About the Company's Public Listing Plans

Listing on Nasdaq under ticker ANT, the AI lab is pitching $17 billion in quarterly revenue alongside $900.9 billion in multi-decade compute obligations.

Quick Answer & Key Takeaway

The company's S-1 registration statement lays out both the bull and bear case in stark numerical terms. Anthropic is set to test public market appetite for frontier artificial intelligence on an unprecedented scale, pricing its initial public offering on November 18 ahead of a November 19, 2026 debut on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT. With a marketed price range of $70 to $78 per share, the company is targeting a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion, which would immediately rank it among the largest publicly traded technology corporations in the world.

Explosive revenue meets massive fixed obligations

The company's S-1 registration statement lays out both the bull and bear case in stark numerical terms. Anthropic generated $17 billion in revenue during the third quarter of 2026, reflecting rapid enterprise adoption of its Claude models across software engineering, financial analysis, and autonomous workflow agents. Reaching a $68 billion annualized revenue pace just a few years after founding gives institutional buyers tangible commercial scale rather than speculative research promises.

Yet the liability side of the filing has sparked intense debate on trading desks. Anthropic disclosed roughly $900.9 billion in long-term compute commitments extending through 2045. Those agreements lock in guaranteed access to massive cloud and accelerator clusters, protecting the lab against supply shortages, but they also create staggering fixed obligations that assume sustained enterprise demand for nearly two decades.

What public shareholders will watch first

Once ANT begins trading, Anthropic will face quarterly discipline that private venture markets rarely enforce. Portfolio managers will scrutinize gross margin expansion as inference workloads scale, customer retention among Fortune 500 buyers, and the company's ability to convert raw API usage into stickier enterprise software contracts.

The listing arrives during a fragile stretch for technology equities, with Federal Reserve officials openly questioning the return timeline on industrywide AI spending. A smooth debut for Anthropic could reopen the IPO window for late-stage AI firms, while a rocky reception would force private investors to rethink lofty valuations across the sector.
