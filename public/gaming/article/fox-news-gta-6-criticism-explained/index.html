Fox News GTA 6 Criticism: What Happened, Rachel Campos-Duffy Lucia Comments, and the Weazel News Irony

Fox News is facing severe backlash after daytime hosts called Lucia a hooker, told adult gamers to get a job, and sparked Weazel News memes.

EB
Ethan Brooks
Senior Gaming Editor
Nov 20, 2026 • 6 min read

The recent commentary from Fox News regarding Grand Theft Auto VI has sparked a massive online reaction across Reddit, X, and TikTok, with millions of gamers and critics pointing out the absurdity and outdated nature of the network's moral panic.

Why is Fox News facing massive criticism over GTA 6?

Fox News is facing massive criticism over GTA 6 because its coverage relied on fear-mongering, unfounded health warnings, and dismissive attacks on adult gamers rather than engaging with the game as a piece of modern entertainment. The network aired multiple segments warning that GTA mania drives fears of dangerous copycat stunts and claimed that the game comes with serious health risks, framing it as a societal danger rather than a complex piece of interactive media. This approach ignores decades of research showing no causal link between video games and real-world violence, echoing discredited moral crusades from the mid-2000s, such as those led by Jack Thompson, which gaming journalists from Variety and Kotaku have long debunked. The community reaction on Reddit was overwhelmingly dismissive, with users pointing out the hypocrisy of a network that often promotes gun ownership while simultaneously warning about game-related violence.

The broader media reaction has been one of weary skepticism, with outlets like The Independent and The Daily Beast noting the predictable nature of this culture war framing. Instead of discussing the game's narrative depth or its cultural impact, the focus remained strictly on sensationalizing potential negative outcomes, such as the comparison made by a panelist to giving a child a gun. This tactic is a tired trope in conservative media, designed to provoke outrage rather than inform the audience about the actual content or cultural significance of a massive global release like GTA 6. Many viewers felt the coverage was not journalism but rather a performance designed to reinforce existing political talking points about youth and morality, which the gaming community found deeply patronizing.

What happened during Rachel Campos-Duffy's on-air segment about Lucia?

Rachel Campos-Duffy asked on live television if the new protagonist Lucia was a hooker, which immediately drew sharp corrections from her fellow panelists who clarified that Lucia is a co-protagonist and an equal partner in the game's criminal narrative. This moment became a viral flashpoint because it demonstrated a lack of basic understanding about the game's character design, reducing a complex, nuanced character to a single, judgmental label on a national platform. The immediate on-air intervention by her colleagues was widely praised by viewers for correcting misinformation in real time, showing a level of professional accountability that contrasted sharply with the initial, ill-informed question.

The clip quickly spread across TikTok and X, where the community reaction was not just one of amusement at the gaffe but also of frustration at the reductive nature of the question. Users shared clips highlighting how Lucia is presented as a capable, central figure in the story, not merely a stereotype. This incident became a microcosm of the network's tendency to simplify complex modern media into easily digestible, often sexist, talking points, which the gaming community found particularly galling given the game's mature themes. The swift correction served as a powerful moment of on-air pushback against casual prejudice.

Why did commentators attack adult gamers over 40?

Commentators attacked adult gamers over 40 because they were attempting to shame mature hobbyists into conforming to outdated societal expectations about work and productivity, a narrative that completely ignores modern demographic realities. One Fox contributor suggested that adults playing video games should simply "grow up and get a job," a statement that was immediately refuted by data from the Entertainment Software Association (ESA). The ESA confirms that the average gamer is actually between 32 and 36 years old, a demographic that is highly employed and possesses significant disposable income, directly contradicting the network's implied narrative of idle, unproductive hobbyists.

The backlash from the community was swift and unified, with working professionals across the gaming sphere calling out the insensitivity of the remarks. On Reddit threads, users shared their careers, stable jobs, and the genuine enjoyment gaming provides as a form of stress relief, not an escape from responsibility. This was not a critique of work ethic but a critique of ageist and classist commentary disguised as moral guidance. The network was essentially targeting a stable, economically active segment of the population with condescending advice, which alienated not just gamers but a broader group of working adults who felt unfairly judged by the segment.

Why are fans calling Fox News the real-life Weazel News?

Fans are calling Fox News the real-life Weazel News because Rockstar Games created the fictional news outlet Weazel News within the Grand Theft Auto series over twenty years ago as a direct, satirical caricature of sensationalist media, and the network's current behavior mirrors that satire perfectly. This is not a new observation; gamers have been pointing out this irony since the game's earlier iterations, recognizing the satirical intent behind the in-game media. When Fox News airs segments claiming the game is a "breeding ground for violence" or demands people stop playing, the parallel to the exaggerated, often unfounded outrage of Weazel News becomes undeniable to those familiar with the franchise's history.

The irony is that the network is performing the exact caricature that Rockstar built into its fictional world to mock media excess. On X, users frequently posted side-by-side comparisons of Fox News talking points next to Weazel News segments, making the comparison clear and humorous rather than purely aggressive. This recognition shows that the gaming community is not just reacting to the game but is critically aware of media tropes, recognizing when real life is simply repeating a long-running satire. It shifts the criticism from "Fox News is wrong" to "Fox News is performing a tired, mocked caricature."

Are claims of GTA 6 health risks and copycat stunts backed by evidence?

No, claims of GTA 6 driving dangerous copycat stunts or causing serious health risks are not backed by credible scientific evidence. The warnings issued by Fox News, such as those citing a former cop, rely on anecdotal fear rather than peer-reviewed research, which consistently shows that media consumption does not cause criminal behavior. Decades of psychological and sociological studies have thoroughly debunked the direct link between playing violent video games and committing violence, a fact that gaming journalists and researchers have repeatedly confirmed.

For readers and gamers, the takeaway is clear: the network's focus on fear is a tactic, not a factual warning. The community response has been to prioritize critical thinking over accepting sensationalism, recognizing this pattern as a rerun of outdated moral panic rather than a genuine public safety concern. Instead of succumbing to the manufactured anxiety, the conversation should focus on the game's artistic merit, its cultural conversation, and the fact that responsible media consumption requires looking past the loudest, most fear-driven voices on cable news.
