GTA 6 vs GTA 5: What's Changed Between the Two Games?

Thirteen years of hardware leaps, a sharper two-lead narrative, and a six-region Florida-inspired state separate Leonida from 2013's Los Santos.

JK
Jordan Kim
Games & Hardware Reporter
Nov 17, 2026 • 4 min read

Few sequels in entertainment carry a thirteen-year gap as wide as the leap between Grand Theft Auto 5 and Grand Theft Auto 6. When GTA 5 launched in September 2013, it was squeezing every last drop out of PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360 hardware with 512 MB of memory. GTA 6 arrives on November 19, 2026 built exclusively for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, immediately signaling a generational shift in world density, lighting, and ambition.

The most visible structural change sits in the protagonist lineup. GTA 5 split its story three ways across Michael, Franklin, and Trevor, leaning into broad West Coast satire and clashing egos. GTA 6 tightens the focus to a duo, Jason and Lucia, trading three-way spectacle for a closer, relationship-driven crime drama anchored by Lucia's debut as the first modern female co-lead.

Leonida vs Southern San Andreas

Geographically, GTA 5 offered one towering metropolis in Los Santos paired with Blaine County's desert and Mount Chiliad. GTA 6 spreads its State of Leonida across six distinct confirmed regions: Vice City, the tropical Leonida Keys, the Everglades-inspired Grassrivers, weathered Port Gellhorn, industrial Ambrosia, and rugged Mount Kalaga National Park. Instead of funneling most activity back into a single downtown bowl, Leonida spreads major landmarks, waterways, and off-road frontiers across the entire state.

The technical jump shows up immediately on your SSD. GTA 5 originally shipped on a single Blu-ray disc under 20 GB on PS3 before ballooning over a decade of Online updates. GTA 6 starts on day one at roughly 212 GB, reflecting far denser city geometry, nightlife interiors like NINE1NINE and Jack of Hearts, and higher-fidelity audio and assets.

Pricing and the familiar PC wait

One reality has shifted in the wallet: GTA 5 debuted at $59.99, whereas GTA 6's Standard Edition launches at $79.99 USD (£69.99 / ₹5,999), with an Ultimate Edition at $99.99. One thing has not changed at all, however. Just like in 2013, PC players are watching console owners move in first with no Windows release date announced yet.
