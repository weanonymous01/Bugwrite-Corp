Meta hit with $887 million fine in Italy over Facebook Android photo access as secondary profiles wind down

European regulators penalized default photo-gallery access while Facebook users face a December 14 deadline to back up supplemental profiles.

ER
Elena Rostova
Senior Infrastructure Correspondent
Nov 17, 2026 • 4 min read

Meta is dealing with major regulatory and product shake-ups around Facebook in mid-November 2026. Italy's data protection authority, the Garante, hit Meta with a €805 million ($887 million) privacy fine over a Facebook Android feature that accessed users' personal photo galleries and embedded metadata by default without obtaining proper, specific consent.

The steep penalty is one of the largest European privacy actions of the year and lands just weeks before Facebook rolls out a sweeping account simplification that will permanently remove millions of secondary user profiles.

Why Italy fined Meta $887 million

European privacy regulators have scrutinized default mobile data collection for years, and the Garante's ruling targets a particularly sensitive boundary: the camera roll. According to the regulator's findings, Facebook's Android implementation pulled gallery access and metadata by default rather than securing clear, opt-in permission tied to a specific sharing action.

The decision arrives alongside separate policy retreat from Meta. Earlier this month, the company paused and withdrew a controversial update to its commercial terms of service after legal experts, publishers, and lawmakers pushed back sharply over language touching AI data scraping. Existing terms remain active while Meta holds further consultations with stakeholders.

Secondary Facebook profiles shut down on December 14

Everyday Facebook users have an immediate deadline of their own to watch. Meta has confirmed that all additional, supplemental Facebook profiles will be permanently deleted starting December 14, 2026. The move ends the feature that allowed people to attach up to four separate side profiles for hobbies, gaming, or local groups under one primary account.

Users who rely on secondary profiles should download copies of any photos, messages, or posts they want to keep well ahead of December 14 and transfer group administrator roles back to their primary profile so community pages are not stranded. On the creator side, Meta also began expanding its automated video content protection tools from Facebook over to Instagram, helping creators detect and manage unauthorized reposts across both apps.
